## Scope and evidence

Based on the-edge and the-edge-product-induct. Business terms are tied to their actual model names, with particular care around product publication and retailer/supplier relationships.

Inspired by the alphabetical term-and-definition approach in the [AWS AI-DLC v2.8.0 glossary](https://github.com/awslabs/aidlc-workflows/blob/v2.8.0/docs/guide/glossary.md). These are organisation/domain companions; AWS remains the reference for lifecycle vocabulary.

**Source-backed** means the inspected source supports the definition. **Interpretation** means business wording or semantics still require a specific decision. Neither label means human-approved or verified in production.

Only committed source was cited. Every cited local file was checked against its recorded commit. Source links require access to the private repositories.

## Naming traps

- **Customer:** The Customer model represents the retailer side; “end customer” means the recipient.
- **Supplier:** suppliers.Supplier and oms_suppliers.Supplier are linked but distinct records.
- **Published:** Product state, transmission and retailer acceptance need separate definitions.
- **Stage / workflow:** PIM publishing and approvals use these words differently from AI-DLC.

## Coverage limits

- Product Induct’s domain and architecture knowledge files contain TODO templates. They were not used as established business definitions.
- The precise PIM-to-Stock & Orders handoff, destination acceptance rules and retailer-specific publishing behaviour require owner review.
- Credit-note semantics, finance policy, customer-specific integration rules and stock calculations need further domain evidence.
- The repositories share legacy concepts and modules. A file’s presence alone does not prove which deployed product owns a capability.
